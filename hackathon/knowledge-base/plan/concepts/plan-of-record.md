ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE. A generic planning governance concept, described for the public demo.

A plan of record is the single approved version of the plan that downstream functions are entitled to rely on. Its authority comes not from being the most recent or the most accurate view, but from having completed the agreed approval step.

Three properties define it.

**It is singular.** At any moment exactly one version holds the status. Working files, personal spreadsheets and in-flight scenarios may all exist, but they carry no authority, and a number sourced from them is not the plan even when it is better.

**It is versioned and immutable.** A published version is never edited in place. A correction produces a new version, and the superseded one remains readable. Without this, a figure quoted in a meeting cannot be reproduced afterwards, and every disagreement becomes unresolvable.

**It is explicitly approved.** Something changed state because a named role accepted it, not because a file was saved. The approval is the moment intent becomes commitment.

The practical consequence for anyone reading a number is that *plan of record* and *latest* are not synonyms. A newer unapproved revision may well be more accurate, but supply planning has already committed materials against the approved one. Quoting the newer figure describes a world nobody is currently building towards.

This is also why a plan version and a data snapshot are different things. The version says which plan was agreed; the snapshot says when the data was captured. A reproducible number needs both.
